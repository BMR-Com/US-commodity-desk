
Grain Stocks 

ISSN: 1949-0925

Released September 30, 2026, by the National Agricultural Statistics Service 
(NASS), Agricultural Statistics Board, United States Department of 
Agriculture (USDA).

Old crop corn stocks in all positions   on September 1, 2026 totaled 2.10 
billion bushels, up 35 percent from September 1, 2025. Of the total, 787 
million bushels are stored on-farm and 1.31 billion bushels were stored 
off-farm. June - August 2026 indicated disappearance is 3.20 billion 
bushels, compared with 3.10 billion bushels during the same period a year ago.

Old crop soybean stocks in all   positions on September 1, 2026 totaled 315 
million bushels, down 3 percent from September 1, 2025. Of the total, 90.4 
million bushels are stored on-farm and 225 million bushels were stored off-farm.
June - August 2026 indicated disappearance is 744 million bushels, compared with
683 million bushels during the same period a year ago.

All wheat stocks in all positions on September 1, 2026 totaled 1.85 billion 
bushels, down 14 percent from September 1, 2025. Of the total, 547 million 
bushels are stored on-farm and 1.30 billion bushels were stored off-farm. 
June - August 2026 indicated disappearance is 608 million bushels, compared 
with 710 million bushels during the same period a year ago.

Barley stocks in all positions on September 1, 2026 totaled 131 million 
bushels, down 13 percent from September 1, 2025. On-farm stocks are estimated 
at 84.7 million bushels, 12 percent below a year ago. Off-farm stocks, at 
46.0 million bushels, are 15 percent below September 2025. The 
June - August 2026 indicated disappearance is 51.4 million bushels, 
17 percent below the same period a year earlier.

Oats stored in all positions on September 1, 2026 totaled 67.5 million 
bushels, 5 percent above the stocks on September 1, 2025. Of the total stocks 
on hand, 42.8 million bushels are stored on farms, 4 percent higher than a 
year ago. Off-farm stocks totaled 24.7 million bushels, 9 percent above the 
previous year. Indicated disappearance during June - August 2026 totaled 
33.4 million bushels, compared with 33.9 million bushels during the same 
period a year ago.

Old crop grain sorghum stored in all positions on September 1, 2026 totaled 
35.0 million bushels, down 13 percent from a year ago. On-farm stocks, at 
3.62 million bushels, are up 43 percent from last year. Off-farm stocks, at 
31.3 million bushels, are down 17 percent from September 1, 2025. The 
June - August 2026 indicated disappearance from all positions is 31.7 million 
bushels, down 47 percent from the same period a year ago.

Old crop sunflower stocks in all positions on September 1, 2026 totaled 
303 million pounds, up 45 percent from a year ago. All stocks stored on farms 
totaled 26.4 million pounds and off-farm stocks totaled 276 million pounds. 
Stocks of oil type sunflower seed are 263 million pounds; of this total, 
21.0 million pounds are on-farm stocks and 242 million pounds are off-farm 
stocks. Non-oil sunflower stocks totaled 39.3 million pounds, with 
5.40 million pounds stored on the farm and 33.9 million pounds stored off the 
farm.

Statistical Methodology

Survey Procedures: The grain stocks estimates in this report are based on 
surveys conducted during the first two weeks of September. Separate surveys 
are conducted to obtain the on-farm and off-farm estimates. The on-farm 
stocks survey is a probability survey that includes a sample of approximately 
66,600 farm operators selected from a list of producers that ensures all 
operations in the United States have a chance to be selected. These producers 
are asked to provide the total quantities of grain stored on their operations 
as of September 1, 2026. This includes all whole grains and oilseeds stored 
whether for feed, seed, or sale as well as any stored under a government 
program.

The off-farm stocks survey is an enumeration of all known commercial grain 
storage facilities. This includes approximately 7,800 facilities with 
11.9 billion bushels of storage capacity. An effort is made to obtain a 
report from all facilities. Reports of stock holdings are normally received 
from operations covering about 90 percent of the capacity. Estimates are made 
for missing facilities to make the survey complete.

Estimation Procedures: On-farm and off-farm survey data are reviewed at the 
State and National levels for reasonableness, consistency with historical 
estimates, and current crop size. After estimates are made for on-farm and 
off-farm stocks, the totals of these two are combined and evaluated using the 
balance sheet approach. This method utilizes other sources of data to check 
the reasonableness of the stocks estimates. Estimates of production, imports, 
exports, crushings, millings, and all other recorded uses of grains and 
oilseeds are reviewed to make sure beginning stocks, production, utilization, 
and ending stocks are within reasonable balance and present the best possible 
estimate of stocks.

Revision Policy: On-farm and off-farm stocks are subject to revision the 
quarter following initial publication and again in the following December 1 
Grain Stocks report published in January each year. Revisions can be made 
when late reports are received, errors are detected in reporting and 
calculations, and production estimates are revised. Estimates will also be 
reviewed following the 5-year Census of Agriculture. No revisions to these 
years will be made after that date.

Reliability: Reliability of the on-farm and off-farm stocks must be treated 
separately because the survey designs for the two surveys are very different. 
The on-farm stocks estimates are subject to sampling variability because all 
operations holding on-farm stocks are not included in the sample. This 
variability, as measured by the relative standard error at the United States 
level, is approximately 2.5 percent for corn, 5.5 percent for soybeans, and 
2.6 percent for all wheat. This means that chances are approximately 95 out 
of 100 that survey estimates for stocks will be within plus or minus 
5.0 percent for corn, 11.0 percent for soybeans, and 5.2 percent for all 
wheat of the value that could be developed by averaging the estimates 
produced from all possible samples selected from the same population and 
surveyed using the same procedures. The relative standard errors for sorghum, 
barley, and oats are 11.7, 4.8, and 5.9 percent, respectively.

Survey indications are also subject to non-sampling errors such as omission, 
duplication, imputation for missing data, and mistakes in reporting, 
recording, and processing the data. Off-farm, as well as on-farm stocks, are 
subject to these types of errors. These errors cannot be measured directly, 
but they are minimized through rigid quality controls in the data collection 
process and a careful review of all reported data for consistency and 
reasonableness.
