What the dairy contracts are

Raw milk is perishable and is never traded on the exchange. What trades is the formula price USDA sets for milk each month, plus the four wholesale products that drive that formula: cheddar cheese, butter, nonfat dry milk (NDM) and dry whey. Every CME dairy future is cash-settled to a USDA number, so futures converge to the cash market by construction. There is no delivery, no storage arbitrage and no basis at expiry, only the gap between today's price and what USDA will announce.

Why the CME spot call matters

CME runs a daily physical auction (block and barrel cheese, butter, NDM, dry whey). Plant sales prices key off these spot prices, so spot moves show up in NDPSR one to two weeks later and then in the class price. Spot is the leading indicator; NDPSR is the settlement mechanism.

Formula regime in force

Since June 2025: 40-lb blocks only (barrels dropped), make allowances raised (cheese 25.19¢, butter 22.72¢, NDM 23.93¢, whey 26.68¢), butterfat recovery 91%, Class I mover back to "higher of". Since December 2025: skim components 3.3% protein, 6.0% other solids, 9.3% nonfat solids. The desk's rebuild reproduces every announced Class III/IV price since 2013 to the cent.

The surprise for newcomers: butter barely moves Class III. Higher butter raises the butterfat price, but the protein formula subtracts butterfat value (cheese yields fat as well as protein), so the two effects nearly cancel. Class III is a cheese-and-whey contract. Class IV is a butter-and-NDM contract, and NDM moves Class IV roughly 0.9× as hard as cheese moves Class III.

Linkages to other markets

  • Grains & oilseeds: corn silage, grain and soybean meal are most of a dairy's feed bill. Cheap feed sustains expansion; a feed rally squeezes margins and slows milk growth 6–18 months out.
  • Cattle: cull dairy cows are a large share of US cow beef (processing/ground beef). High cull-cow prices make culling profitable, which speeds herd contraction. Beef-on-dairy crossbred calves are now a major profit line for dairies and a growing source of fed cattle, so dairy and beef herd decisions are linked.
  • Global dairy: NDM trades against EU and NZ skim milk powder, and whey against EU whey. Global Dairy Trade auctions and EU quotations set the export parity the US must meet.
  • Fats & oils: butterfat competes with vegetable oils in food manufacturing and with EU butter globally. Cream is pulled between butter, ice cream and cream cheese seasonally.
  • Macro: USD/MXN (Mexico is the largest buyer of US NDM and cheese), the US dollar for export competitiveness, energy and freight costs (drying is energy-intensive), and interest rates (cost of carrying cheese inventory).

Seasonality

Spring flush (Mar–Jun) is peak milk, so butter and powder stocks build. Production dips in late summer on heat stress, then Q4 holiday demand draws butter and cheese stocks down. Class III and component futures carry this seasonality in the curve shape.
