PLTM

GraniteShares Platinum Trust
Fund Objective

The GraniteShares Platinum Trust is designed to seek the performance of the price of platinum, less trust expenses.

Key Features

 Physically Backed: The Trust holds only LPPM1 good delivery bullion stored in a vault domiciled in London, UK.

 Transparent and Secured: The list of platinum bullion held by the Trust is published daily. The vault is audited twice a year. Lending of the metal is not permitted, and the Trust cannot hold derivatives.

 Cost Effective: PLTM is the lowest cost platinum ETF on the market.2

 Easy to Access: PLTM is listed on NYSE Arca and can be traded through a normal brokerage account.

Multiple Industrial Applications

Platinum has numerous industrial applications in, for example, biomedical research, specialty electronics and green technologies.

Shares of the Trust are not insured by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (“FDIC”), may lose value and have no bank guarantee. PLTM is not a mutual fund or any other type of Investment Company within the meaning of the Investment Company Act of 1940, as amended, and is not subject to regulation thereunder.

The Trust is not a commodity pool for purposes of the Commodity Exchange Act of 1936, as amended

Trust shares trade like stocks, are subject to investment risk and will fluctuate in market value. The value of Trust shares relates directly to the value of the platinum held by the Trust (less its expenses), and fluctuations in the price of gold could materially and adversely affect an investment in the shares. The price received upon the sale of the shares, which trade at market price, may be more or less than the value of the gold represented by them. Shares of the Trust are bought and sold at market price. Brokerage commissions will reduce returns.

Market Price: The current price at which shares are bought and sold. Market returns are based upon the last trade price. NAV: The dollar value of a single share, based on the value of the underlying assets of the Trust minus its liabilities, divided by the number of shares outstanding. Calculated at the end of each business day

Physical Replication: The Trust owns the underlying assets of the index whether they are stocks, bonds, or in this case, platinum bars.

The objective of the Trust is for the value of the Shares to reflect, at any given time, the value of the assets owned by the Trust at that time less the Trust’s accrued expenses and liabilities as of that time. The Shares are intended to constitute a simple and cost-effective means of making an investment similar to an investment in platinum. An investment in allocated physical platinum bullion requires expensive and sometimes complicated arrangements in connection with the assay, transportation and warehousing of the metal. Traditionally, such expense and complications have resulted in investments in physical platinum bullion being efficient only in amounts beyond the reach of many investors. The Shares have been designed to remove the obstacles represented by the expense and complications involved in an investment in physical platinum bullion, while at the same time having an intrinsic value that reflects, at any given time, the price of the assets owned by the Trust at such time less the Trust expenses and liabilities. Although the Shares are not the exact equivalent of an investment in platinum, they provide investors with an alternative that allows a level of participation in the platinum market through the securities market.

Trust Risks

Investing in the shares involves significant risks, including possible loss of principal. You could lose money on an investment in the Trust. For a more complete discussion of risk factors relative to the Trust, carefully read the prospectus.

Shares are created to reflect the price of the platinum held by the Trust, the market price of the shares will be as unpredictable as the price of platinum has historically been. This creates the potential for losses, regardless of whether you hold Shares for the short-, mid- or long-term.

The amount of platinum represented by each share will decrease over the life of the Trust due to the sales of platinum necessary to pay the Sponsor’s Fee and Trust expenses.

Without increases in the price of platinum sufficient to compensate for that decrease, the price of the Shares will also decline and you will lose money on your investment in shares

The Trust is a passive investment vehicle. The price received upon the sale of shares may be less than the value of the platinum represented by them.

The Trust is not a diversified investment, it may be more volatile than other investments.

The Trust may be forced to sell platinum earlier than anticipated if expenses are higher than expected.

12-Month Trailing Yield:

The yield an investor would have received if they held the fund over the last 12 months, based on the most recent net asset value (NAV). It is calculated by summing income, capital gains, and return of capital distributions paid over the past 12 months, and dividing that by the most recent NAV plus any capital distributions made over the same period.

Bid/Ask Spread:

The difference between the highest price a buyer is willing to pay (bid) and the lowest price a seller is willing to accept (ask). It is an indicator of liquidity and trading costs.

Business Development Companies (BDCs):

Publicly traded investment firms that provide capital to small and mid-sized businesses, often in the form of debt or equity. BDCs are required to distribute most of their income to shareholders to qualify for favorable tax treatment.

Closed-End Funds (CEFs):

Pooled investment vehicles that raise a fixed amount of capital through an IPO and then list their shares for trading on an exchange. Unlike open-end funds, CEFs do not issue or redeem shares daily.

Daily Reset:

A feature of leveraged and inverse ETFs where the fund seeks to deliver a specific multiple of the underlying asset’s daily performance. Because of this daily reset, returns over longer periods may deviate significantly from the expected multiple of the benchmark performance.

Derivatives:

Financial contracts whose value is based on the performance of an underlying asset, index, or rate. Common derivatives include options, futures, and swaps. Derivatives are often used to gain leverage or hedge risk.

ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund):

A pooled investment vehicle that holds a basket of assets (stocks, bonds, etc.) and trades on a stock exchange like a single security. ETFs offer intraday liquidity and typically track an index or strategy.

Gold Benchmark – LBMA Gold Price PM:

A globally recognized benchmark for gold pricing administered by ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA). It is based on the afternoon (PM) auction for the London Bullion Market.

Intended Audience:

Refers to the investor type or classification for whom a financial product or content is designed such as retail investors, professional clients, or qualified investors.

Long Position:

An investment strategy that seeks to profit from a rise in the price of a security. In leveraged ETFs, a “long” fund might offer 2x or 3x the daily performance of the underlying asset.

Margin Call:

A request from a broker for an investor to deposit additional funds or securities into a margin account to cover potential losses.

Market Maker:

A firm or dealer that provides liquidity by continuously offering to buy and sell securities at publicly quoted prices. Market makers help reduce spreads and facilitate trading.

Market Price:

The current trading price at which a security or ETF share is bought or sold on an exchange.

Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs):

Publicly traded partnerships that primarily operate in energy and natural resources. MLPs combine the tax benefits of partnerships with the liquidity of publicly traded securities.

NAV (Net Asset Value):

The per-share value of a fund, calculated by subtracting liabilities from total assets and dividing by the number of shares outstanding. NAV is typically calculated at the end of each trading day.

Options-Based Strategy:

A fund strategy that involves the use of options such as selling covered calls to generate income, manage volatility, or potentially enhance performance. Common in income-generating ETFs like GraniteShares’ YieldBoost series.

Professional Investor / Qualified Investor:

A classification used in various jurisdictions to define investors who meet specific criteria related to financial expertise, experience, and resources. Products may be restricted to these groups depending on local regulations.

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs):

Companies that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate. REITs must distribute a significant portion of their income to shareholders and are used by investors seeking real estate exposure and dividend income.

Return of Capital (ROC):

ROC indicates how much the distribution reflects an investor's initial investment. Short Position:

A strategy that seeks to profit from a decline in the price of a security. Inverse ETFs provide short exposure to an underlying asset or index, sometimes with leverage.

Tracking Error:

The difference between the performance of an ETF and its underlying benchmark or index. In leveraged and inverse ETFs, tracking error may be greater due to the effects of compounding and daily resets.

Volatility:

A measure of the degree of variation in an asset’s price over time. Higher volatility indicates a wider range of potential price outcomes and greater risk.

Leveraged ETF

A type of exchange‑traded fund designed to provide 2x or 3x the daily performance of an underlying index, commodity, or single stock. These funds use financial instruments and leverage, reset daily, and are intended for short-term trading, not long-term holding.

Inverse ETF

An ETF structured to provide the opposite of the daily performance of its underlying asset or index (e.g., -1×, -2×, -3×). These funds are used to profit from or hedge against a downward market move. Like leveraged ETFs, they rebalance daily and carry higher risk over extended periods.

Volatility Decay

Also known as volatility drag. This occurs in leveraged or inverse ETFs due to the daily reset mechanism, where the compounding of repeated daily performance especially in volatile markets can erode value over time, even if the underlying asset’s long-term trend is flat or upward.

YieldBoost ETFs

A range of GraniteShares ETFs that use a put spread strategy to generate high income. This involves selling near-the-money put options on leveraged ETFs and buying further out-of-the-money puts to limit downside risk. YieldBoost ETFs are not leveraged themselves but benefit from the higher premiums on options linked to leveraged ETFs. The strategy distributes income weekly and seeks to offer enhanced yields with built-in risk control.

Option(s)

A contract granting the buyer the right but not the obligation to buy (call) or sell (put) an underlying asset at a specified price before a set expiration date. GraniteShares uses options in certain strategies to generate income, manage exposure, or provide enhanced yield.

Put Option

The right, but not the obligation, to sell an underlying asset at a predetermined price within a specified time period. Often used for hedging or income strategies.

Covered‑Call Strategy

An income-focused investment approach that involves holding a long position in an underlying asset (such as an ETF or stock) and selling call options on that asset. GraniteShares uses this strategy in products like YieldBoost to capture premiums and generate additional yield.

Call Option

The right, but not the obligation, to buy an underlying asset at a predetermined price within a specified time period.

Flex Options

Customized options contracts traded on an exchange but with flexible terms such as expiration date, strike price, and settlement style. Commonly used by institutional investors.

30-Day SEC Yield:

The 30-Day SEC Yield represents the net investment income (excluding option income) earned by the ETF over the 30-day period ended [Most recent day the 30-SEC was provided]. It is expressed as an annualized percentage rate based on the ETF’s share price at the end of that period. This metric does not reflect the total income generated by the fund, as it excludes option premium income central to the YieldBOOST strategy.

Distribution Rate

The Distribution Rate shown is as of based of the NAV per share as of [day before declaration date] adjusted for corporate actions. the Distribution Rate is the annual rate an investor would receive if the most recent distribution remained the same going forward. The rate represents a single distribution from the fund and does not represent total return to the fund. The distribution rate is calculated by annualizing the most recent distribution and dividing it by the most recent NAV adjusted for corporate actions.

Ex-Date (Ex-Dividend Date)

The date on which an ETF trades without the value of its next distribution. Investors must purchase shares before the ex-date to be eligible to receive the upcoming distribution. Purchases on or after the ex-date will not qualify for the payout.

Record Date

The date set by the fund to determine which shareholders are entitled to receive a declared distribution. Investors listed as shareholders on the record date having purchased before the ex-date will receive the payout.

Pay Date

The date on which the ETF pays out the distribution to eligible shareholders. YieldBoost ETFs typically distribute income weekly, though payment schedules may vary based on fund structure and calendar timing.

Volatility

A statistical measure of the dispersion of returns for a given asset. Higher volatility indicates greater price fluctuations and is a key consideration in options pricing.

Liquidity

The ease with which an asset can be bought or sold in the market without affecting its price. ETFs typically have two layers of liquidity: the liquidity of the ETF shares and the underlying assets.

Expense Ratio

The annual fee expressed as a percentage of assets that investors pay for fund management and operations. It impacts the net return to investors.

Creation/Redemption Mechanism

The process by which ETFs issue and redeem shares in large blocks (creation units) through authorized participants, helping to keep the ETF’s market price close to its NAV.

Risk Disclosure

Information provided to investors about the potential risks involved in an investment product. This is a regulatory requirement, especially for complex products like leveraged or options-based ETFs.
