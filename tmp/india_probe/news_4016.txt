            {
                nStr += '';
                x = nStr.split('.');
                x1 = x[0];
                x2 = x.length > 1 ? '.' + x[1] : '';
                var rgx = /(\d+)(\d{3})/;

                var n1, n2;
                num = num + '' || '';
                // works for integer and floating as well

                n1 = n1[0].replace(/(\d)(?=(\d\d)+\d$)/g, "$1,");
                num = n2 ? n1 + '.' + n2 : n1;
                return num;
            }

It provides interest subvention, operational expenditure support, and testing and certification assistance. Targets include capacity of up to 7.5 lakh TEUs, 3,000 direct jobs and over 50,000 indirect jobs.</p><p class="ql-align-justify">India’s Union Cabinet, chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, has approved the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme (CMPS) with a budgetary outlay of Rs 10,000 crore ($1.05 billion) up to FY 2039-40 to build a container manufacturing ecosystem in India, reduce import dependence and strengthen supply-chain resilience, according to the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways. The scheme is intended to support both new manufacturing facilities and the expansion of existing container manufacturing units. The Scheme for Container Manufacturing was announced in the Union Budget 2026-27 CMPS includes Interest Subvention Assistance with a financial outlay of Rs 859 crore ($90.1 million). This will provide interest subvention of 3 per cent per annum on eligible term loans, or capital loans, availed by container manufacturers for setting up greenfield container manufacturing facilities and brownfield expansion of existing capacity.</p><p class="ql-align-justify">The largest component is Operational Expenditure Assistance, with an outlay of Rs 8,891 crore ($933 million). It will provide financial assistance for containers manufactured as per global shipping standards and sold by eligible container manufacturers, with the aim of bridging the cost gap and improving the competitiveness of domestically manufactured containers.</p><p class="ql-align-justify">The scheme also provides testing and certification assistance with an outlay of Rs 150 crore ($15.7 million) to support common facilities, including dynamic longitudinal impact testing, the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways said in a press release.</p><p class="ql-align-justify">The ministry said the scheme is envisaged to increase annual domestic container manufacturing capacity by around ten-fold to up to 7.5 lakh TEUs. It is also intended to create an internationally competitive manufacturing base with potential to position India as a global export hub for quality shipping containers.</p><p class="ql-align-justify">CMPS is expected to strengthen India’s maritime and logistics supply chains, generate an estimated 3,000 direct jobs and over 50,000 indirect jobs, and support ancillary industries such as corner castings, specialised steel, wooden frames and paints.</p><p class="ql-align-justify"><strong>&nbsp;</strong></p><p class="ql-align-justify"><strong>ICE cotton futures edge higher on bargain buying, mill demand</strong></p><p>Thursday, 29th Oct 2026, (Source: <a href="http://www.fibre2fashion.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">www.fibre2fashion.com</a>)</p><p class="ql-align-justify"><strong>Insights:</strong> ICE December 2026 cotton futures settled 0.12 per cent higher at 78.15 cents per pound on bargain hunting and textile mill buying after four sessions of losses.Abundant supplies, a firm US dollar, lower open interest and higher stocks capped gains as technical weakness persisted.Markets are watching the Federal Reserve, US harvest progress and a possible India duty-free import extension. ICE cotton futures edged higher on Wednesday after four consecutive sessions of losses, supported by bargain hunting and increased buying from textile mills. However, abundant supplies and a stronger US dollar limited gains. The most active December 2026 contract gained 9 points, or 0.12 per cent, to settle at 78.15 cents per pound, compared with 78.06 cents in the previous session. Despite the recovery, the contract remained down 368 points over the past five sessions. Other cotton contracts settled 8–25 points higher.Trading volume declined to 54,076 contracts from 64,652 in the previous session. Open interest fell by 780 contracts to 397,988, marking its fourth consecutive decline after reaching a record 402,264 contracts on October 21. ICE certificated cotton stocks increased to 27,591 bales as of October 27, compared with 26,791 bales in the previous trading session. Market analysts noted that the December contract traded between 77.61 and 78.56 cents during the session, recording a range of 95 points. The contract formed a lower high and a lower low for the fourth consecutive session and closed below its 200-day moving average for the second successive day, indicating continued technical weakness. Cotton futures initially slipped to their lowest level since October 8 before recovering on buying at lower prices. Market analysts said that buyers were returning to the market following four consecutive sessions of declines. Textile mills were also showing greater interest in purchasing cotton at current price levels. Although demand has not improved significantly, it remains stable and is gradually recovering. Meanwhile, the US dollar remained firm after touching a two-week high ahead of the Federal Reserve's interest rate decision. A stronger dollar makes US cotton more expensive for overseas buyers, weighing on export competitiveness. Market sentiment was also influenced by reports that India was considering extending its duty-free cotton import policy until December 2026, as domestic cotton production was expected to fall short of earlier estimates. The existing duty-free import arrangement is scheduled to expire on October 31. An extension could help textile mills secure raw materials at lower costs, although no official announcement has been made. In related markets, CBOT soybean futures recovered for the first time in three sessions, supported by bargain hunting and reduced production estimates for Brazil. However, harvesting pressure limited gains. Crude oil prices also increased by approximately 1 per cent, potentially supporting cotton through higher costs for competing synthetic fibres. Market participants are now monitoring support at 77.05 cents, followed by 76.87 cents, while resistance is seen at 79.23 cents and subsequently at 79.80–80.00 cents. The Federal Reserve's decision, dollar movements, US harvest progress, India's cotton import policy and upcoming index fund rolls are expected to influence cotton futures in the coming sessions. This morning (Indian Standard Time), December 2026 cotton traded at 77.90 cents per pound (down 0.25 cent). Cash cotton traded at 75.90 cents (up 0.09 cent), while the March 2027 contract traded at 81.68 cents (down 0.22 cent). His later remarks raised concerns about dependence on technology and autonomous physical systems; these were expressed as concerns about future use rather than established outcomes of textile deployments.</p><p><strong>ITAMMA's Digital Transformation and Smart Data Clinic</strong></p><h3>N D Mhatre: ITAMMA's Digital Journey</h3><p>ITAMMA Director General Technical N D Mhatre described the association's digital transformation journey since 2022. He contrasted the reach of physical events and printed information with the continuing digital connections among members, technology providers and the wider textile ecosystem.</p><h3>Ganesh Rajagopal: Industry Intelligence and Data Access</h3><p>Continuing the joint closing presentation, Rajagopal discussed mobile access to knowledge and the collection, curation and analysis of industry information.</p><p>Demonstrations included member geography and trade information, with the longer-term direction moving towards enterprise-specific intelligence. The discussion also covered planned additions; the presentation material did not establish that every mentioned dashboard or data source was already operational.</p><h3>Dhandayuthapani Chidambaram: Vote of Thanks</h3><p>Dhandayuthapani Chidambaram, introduced as Managing Director of MAG Solvics and an ITAMMA committee member, delivered the vote of thanks. He acknowledged the sponsor, supporting association, knowledge partners, media partners, moderators, speakers and participants.</p><p><strong>Key Takeaways for Textile Businesses</strong></p><p>The conference discussions pointed to several practical considerations for companies exploring AI adoption:</p><ul><li><strong>Start with a defined problem:</strong> Identify a recurring operational or commercial issue before selecting a technology.</li><li><strong>Establish a baseline:</strong> Compare performance before and after implementation to determine whether an application delivers measurable benefits.</li><li><strong>Build on reliable data:</strong> Check data accuracy, relevance and accessibility, including information from machines and existing business systems.</li><li><strong>Evaluate costs and benefits:</strong> Consider raw materials, quality, downtime, resource consumption, inventory and market response.</li><li><strong>Retain textile expertise:</strong> Operators, managers, researchers and students need the skills to assess outputs, identify errors and make responsible decisions.</li><li><strong>Encourage industry–academic collaboration:</strong> Shared learning, relevant datasets and focused pilot projects can support the development of applications suited to textile operations.</li></ul><p>These conclusions reflect the themes of the conference discussions rather than a formally adopted conference resolution.</p><p>The event underscored that AI adoption in textiles involves more than installing new software or automated machinery. Its practical value depends on how effectively companies connect technology with operational knowledge, reliable information, trained personnel and measurable business objectives.</p>							<a href="https://www.caionline.in/uploads/cards/doc/cn_engish_30_0425-29_10_2026.pdf" class="download-btn" target="_blank"><img src="https://www.caionline.in/web/public/images/downloads.png" alt="Daily News Related to Cotton & Textile Sector."></a>						</div>
